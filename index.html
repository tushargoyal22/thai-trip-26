The route: city first, then island-hop south

You fly once inside Thailand, from Bangkok down to Krabi. From there, ferries carry you across the Andaman Sea to Phi Phi and then Phuket, and you fly home from Phuket. No backtracking, and every leg is short.

Three beach moods, not one

Ao Nang is your easy base with limestone cliffs. Phi Phi is the party island with the famous lagoons. Phuket brings bigger beaches, Old Town and the loudest nightlife.

The cost: more hotel changes

That’s four hotels in ten nights, with a move every 2–3 nights. That’s the limit for a relaxed trip, so don’t add any more destinations. Pack one medium bag each, not big suitcases. Ferries and Phi Phi’s car-free lanes punish heavy luggage.

What about Koh Samui, Phangan or Tao?

These islands sit on the Gulf of Thailand side, the opposite coast from Krabi and Phuket. They’re lovely, but they belong to a different route, so the main plan sticks to the Andaman side. Phi Phi on Christmas Eve is a great party night in its own right, with beach bars, fire shows and DJs. That keeps Krabi and Phi Phi, the group’s must-haves, and avoids crossing coasts. If anyone really wants Koh Phangan’s Full Moon Party on Thu 24 Dec, it can still be done as a short detour from Krabi.

Full Moon Party detour: out on the 24th, Phi Phi on the 25th

For anyone willing to travel, this is the way to do it:

  • 24 Dec, morning: take a bus-and-ferry combo from Krabi to Koh Phangan (~5–7 hours), arriving by evening.
  • That night: party on Haad Rin beach until dawn. No hotel needed: Phangan rooms on Full Moon night are very expensive and often require several nights.
  • 25 Dec, early morning: take a combined ferry–bus–ferry ticket straight to Phi Phi. The earliest leave Thong Sala pier around 05:00 and arrive around 15:30, in time for Christmas night with the group. Sleep on the bus.
  • Bags: the rest of the group takes the Phangan crew’s big bags to Phi Phi on the 24th, so you travel with just a daypack. No Ao Nang room needed.
  • Backup: if a connection runs late, stay the night in Krabi or Ao Nang and take the morning ferry to Phi Phi on the 26th.
  • Book both directions in advance, because the ferries after the party are packed. Check times

How it fits with the rest of the plan

  • If only some of you go: the rest follow the plan on Phi Phi, and the Phangan crew joins them there on the afternoon of the 25th for Christmas night. Everyone takes the 26th ferry to Phuket together.
  • If everyone goes: leave Ao Nang with your bags on the morning of the 24th, and take the same Phangan → Phi Phi ticket on the 25th. Phi Phi becomes one night (the 25th) instead of two.
  • The trade-off: two long travel days and a sleepless night, in exchange for one legendary party, and less time on Phi Phi.

Fitting Samui or Tao in as proper stops would add a fifth stop and a full travel day, so they’re better saved for a separate trip.

How we got here: the three options we compared

Bangkok 3, Chiang Mai 3, Krabi 4. The broadest trip, but two airport half-days, a scarce Chiang Mai → Krabi flight, and a Monday Krabi return risk.

Option 2: sleeper train north

Same as Option 1 but with an overnight train to Chiang Mai. A good story to tell, but sleep is hit-and-miss and December berths sell out.

One flight, more beach and nightlife, now extended into a Krabi → Phi Phi → Phuket loop. You give up the elephants, northern temples and the cooler weather.

Leaving Bangkok on Sun 20 instead? Two options

The plan below flies to Krabi on Monday morning. If the group would rather head south on Sunday, these are the two ways that work. A Sunday midday flight isn’t recommended: it costs you Chatuchak (weekends only) and still saves nothing.

Option A: overnight VIP bus

Saves a hotel night. Costs a night of real sleep.

  • From Bangkok’s Southern Bus Terminal (Sai Tai Mai). Leave around 19:00 and arrive in Krabi around 06:00, about 11–12 hours.
  • Around THB 700–1,150 per person, and you skip the Sunday Bangkok hotel night.
  • Choose a VIP 24 bus (three seats per row). Avoid services that arrive around 03:40.
  • Leave Chatuchak by ~17:00. The terminal is on the west side of the city, ~200 THB by taxi plus traffic.
  • You arrive at Krabi bus terminal, 30–40 min from Ao Nang and hours before check-in. Plan a café-and-beach morning, or pay for early check-in.
  • Book seats early, because Christmas week sells out. Check times

Option B: Sunday evening flight

More beach time. No money saved.

  • Keep Chatuchak and Sunday afternoon as planned. Skip only the night market.
  • Fly around 19:00–20:30 from Don Mueang or Suvarnabhumi (~1h20), and reach Ao Nang around 22:30.
  • Monday becomes a full day in Ao Nang, making it 4 nights there.
  • No saving: the Sunday night moves from Bangkok to Krabi, which usually costs more in Christmas week.

Practical tips

What saves money, time and awkward moments.

Money: cash, cards and rates

The default rate is only a placeholder. Check today’s rate and type it in.

  • Cash runs markets, street food, longtails, massages, island bars and tuk-tuks. Cards work in malls, hotels, chains and most restaurants.
  • Phi Phi is cash-heavy. Arrive with enough for bars, food and the boat to Maya Bay. There are ATMs, but queues are long at peak times.
  • Thai ATMs charge a flat fee (around THB 220) per foreign-card withdrawal, on top of your bank’s fees, so take out larger amounts less often. Check
  • A multi-currency forex card (Niyo, BookMyForex, HDFC/ICICI) usually beats exchanging rupees. If you carry cash, exchange it at SuperRich in Bangkok rather than the airport.
  • When a card machine offers to charge in INR, always choose THB. Paying in INR (dynamic currency conversion) adds a hidden markup.
  • Rough daily spend excluding hotels, at mid-range comfort: about THB 1,500–2,500 per person. Boat tours and big nights push it up. Estimate
SIM, eSIM and data
  • For iPhones and recent Pixels and Galaxys, the easiest option is an eSIM bought before you fly (Airalo, Holafly, or AIS/TrueMove tourist eSIMs). Activate it at the Delhi gate.
  • For a physical SIM, the AIS and TrueMove counters in the arrivals hall sell tourist packs with your passport. Queues are long at 21:00 in peak week.
  • Thai tourist packs include a local number, which helps for Grab, ferry pickups and restaurant bookings. Pure data eSIMs don’t.
  • Coverage is good on Phi Phi Don and Phuket. It drops at sea and on some outer islands.
Getting around: Grab, boats, island logistics
  • Bangkok: Grab or Bolt (book XL for four people with bags), plus the BTS and MRT. Take the Chao Phraya Express boat to the temples.
  • Ao Nang: buy longtail tickets at the official beach counter (fixed prices, departs when about 8 seats fill). Boats thin out after dark.
  • Phi Phi: there are no cars. You walk everywhere, and porters with carts meet the ferry. Longtail taxis go to other beaches. Carry light bags.
  • Phuket: taxis and tuk-tuks are expensive and don’t use meters. Use Grab or Bolt where they’ll pick up, and agree fixed prices otherwise. Patong to Kata is ~20 min.
  • Ferries: book combined tickets with hotel pickup. Arrive 30 min early. Keep valuables and passports on you, not in the luggage pile on deck.
Temple dress code
  • Shoulders and knees covered for everyone. The Grand Palace is the strictest: no sleeveless tops, shorts, ripped jeans or see-through fabric. The same rule applies at Ayutthaya’s active temples and Phuket’s Big Buddha.
  • Each of you should pack one pair of light trousers and a T-shirt or shirt. Sarongs are sold outside temples, but queuing for one wastes your morning.
  • Shoes off inside temple halls. Don’t point your feet at Buddha images, and don’t climb on ruins for photos at Ayutthaya.
  • Never joke about or disrespect the King or royal family, even in pictures or memes. It’s a serious criminal offence.
Scams to avoid
  • “The Grand Palace is closed today.” It isn’t. A friendly stranger then steers you to a cheap tuk-tuk, a “lucky Buddha” and a gem or suit shop. Walk on.
  • Jet ski “damage” claims, especially in Patong. If you rent one, photograph every scratch first. Better still, skip them.
  • Bar bills and “shows” on Bangla Road and in Patpong. Touts promise free entry, then a huge bill arrives. Check drink prices first and pay each round.
  • Taxis that won’t use the meter in Bangkok. Get out and open Grab instead. In Phuket, agree the fare before getting in.
  • Unlicensed boats. Book through your hotel, Klook or GetYourGuide, and check for life jackets and a national park fee receipt.
  • Drinks and buckets at Phi Phi and Bangla Road parties. Watch your drinks. Buckets are strong. Stick together and agree a meeting point.
  • Fire-rope jumping and skipping games on Phi Phi beaches cause real burns every season. Watch, don’t play, after a few buckets.
Laws that catch Indian travellers out
  • Vapes and e-cigarettes are illegal to bring in, buy or use. Police do street checks in Patong and Bangkok, and fines are steep. Leave them in Delhi.
  • Cannabis: despite the shops you’ll see, the rules were tightened in 2025 and recreational buying isn’t legal. Don’t risk it, and never carry any home. Rules change
  • Stores sell alcohol only 11:00–14:00 and 17:00–24:00. Bars and restaurants operate on their own licences.
  • Clubs often check ID, so carry a photo of your passport, or the passport itself on big nights.
  • Scooters: an Indian licence without an International Driving Permit voids most insurance, and police checkpoints fine riders without helmets. On Phuket’s hills, Grab is safer.
Weather, sea and packing
  • December is dry season on the Andaman coast: calm, clear water and prime time for boat trips. Expect ~30–32°C everywhere.
  • Pack reef-safe sunscreen, a dry bag for boat days, water shoes or grippy sandals (Railay rocks, the Phi Phi viewpoint), sunglasses with a strap, and a power bank. Power banks go in cabin baggage only.
  • Speedboats bounce. Anyone prone to seasickness should take a tablet 30 min before the Maya Bay and Hong Islands trips.
Entry: TDAC and what immigration may ask
  • Fill in the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card at tdac.immigration.go.th within the 3 days before arrival, so on 16, 17 or 18 Dec. Avoid paid look-alike sites.
  • Officers can ask for your return ticket, hotel bookings and proof of funds (about THB 10,000 per person). Keep PDFs offline on your phone, including your Phuket–Delhi booking.
  • Your passport needs at least 6 months’ validity.

Honest take

The right trip for this group, and now properly relaxed.

Bangkok gets three fun nights covering the essentials. The south gets seven, with a move only every two or three days, and the trip ends at the airport you fly home from. There’s no day with more than one boat or flight.

The one real cost is Phi Phi over Christmas. Two nights there are the priciest and scarcest rooms of the trip, so book them before anything else.

Want Ayutthaya back?

Do a half-day there on Sunday morning (leave 07:30, back ~13:30) and move Chatuchak to the afternoon. It’s hot and a bit rushed, so only if someone really wants it.

Phi Phi too pricey?

Drop to one night there (24 Dec), take the ferry to Phuket on Christmas Day and give Phuket a third night. You keep the Christmas Eve party.

Want more party?

Stay in Patong instead of Kata. Bangla Road is a walk from your door, but expect noise until 3am and a beach that’s worse for swimming.
